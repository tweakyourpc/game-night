Checkers here follows American / English draughts for two players, plus one platform rule (below). You see your own pieces at the bottom of the board.

Setup and moving

  • The board is 8 × 8 and play uses only the dark squares. Each player starts with 12 pieces. Dark moves first.
  • A piece (a "man") moves one square diagonally forward to an empty square.
  • A man that reaches the far row is crowned and becomes a king (shown with ♛). Kings move one square diagonally forward or backward.

Capturing

  • Capture by jumping diagonally over an opposing piece next to yours onto the empty square beyond it. The jumped piece is removed. Men capture forward only; kings capture in any diagonal direction.
  • Capturing is compulsory. When you can capture, only the pieces that can capture are highlighted. If several captures are possible you may choose any of them; you do not have to take the most pieces.
  • After a capture, if the same piece can capture again, it must keep jumping. The piece stays selected and pulses until the sequence is over.
  • Crowning ends your turn, even in the middle of a sequence of jumps.

Winning, resigning and draws

  • You win when your opponent has no pieces left, or cannot make a legal move on their turn.
  • You may resign at any time; your opponent wins.
  • You may offer a draw. The game is drawn only if your opponent accepts. If they make a move instead of answering, the offer lapses.
  • Platform rule (not part of the traditional rules): if the same position, with the same player to move, occurs for the third time, the game is drawn.

Making a move

Tap or click one of your highlighted pieces, then one of the highlighted squares (a ring marks a capture). With a keyboard, press Tab to reach the pieces and squares you can use, then Enter. Squares are named a1 to h8 from Dark's side.

Table option

Who plays Dark (moves first): the first seat (usually the host), or decided at random when the game starts.
